> Inspirations: *Goblin Slayer* (tone, goblins as the only enemy, an adventurers' guild

## The world in one page

Under the world lies **the Below**. Nobody has seen its floor. What comes up from it is
goblins.

Where the Below touches the surface, the ground splits into a **Rift**: a crack in a
hillside, a cellar that was not so deep the day before, a well that no longer holds
water. A Rift leads to a nest. Goblins do not travel to a place; they arrive under it.

The Below does not obey time. Inside a Rift, and in the wild lands where Rifts are many,
**nothing moves unless the living move**. Adventurers call it **the Hush**. Stand still
in a nest and the torches do not flicker, the goblin in front of you does not blink; take
a step and everything takes a step with you. Veterans say the Hush is the only mercy the
Below ever showed, and that it was not meant as one.

Walls keep the Hush out. Inside a town, time runs as it should. This is why people live
in towns, why roads between them are dangerous, and why a farm beyond the wall is a bet.

A Rift does not stay. It opens, it **ripens**, and if nobody kills what rules the nest, it
**spills**: the goblins come out, all of them, and the nearest village learns what the
Guild is for. Kill the ruler, the **Heart** of the nest, and the Rift begins to close.
Next season another opens somewhere else, and its tunnels are never the same.

## Stillstone

Rifts grow a crystal that exists nowhere else: **stillstone**. It forms in veins along
the walls of a nest, denser the deeper and older the nest. It holds a little of the Hush.

It is **rare**: a nest holds a handful of stones, not a seam. Nobody builds with it.
It is used where something must be undone or held without breaking:

| Craft | What a stone does |
|---|---|
| Smiths | Binds a weapon to the one hand that will carry it |
| Enchanters | Lifts a working off a blade without shattering the blade |
| Alchemists | Holds a brew still long enough to read what it wants to become |

The real wealth of a nest is what the goblins carry: what they stole, what they made, and
what is left of those who came before. Stillstone is what the craftsmen ask for when
nothing else will do.

The ruler of a nest carries the largest stone of all, grown in its body: the
**heartstone**. Bringing one back is proof that a Rift is closed.

## The Guild

The **Adventurers' Guild** was founded by villagers who were tired of burying their own,
not by knights. It posts what needs killing, pays for proof, and decides who may enter
which Rift.

### Grades

The Guild's **assayers** grade a Rift from outside, by the colour and weight of the
stillstone dust at its mouth. The grade is the name of a metal, and it is the same ladder
as the tags adventurers wear:

> Wood · Tin · Copper · Iron · Steel · Bronze · Silver · Gold · Platinum · Onyx

A Copper tag means the Guild trusts you to come back from a Copper Rift. The tag is made
of the material; a Wood tag is a chip of wood on a string, and everyone starts with one.
The last tag, Onyx, is cut from stillstone itself, which is black. Few have seen one.

Assayers are right most of the time.

### Red Rifts

Sometimes the dust at the mouth of a Rift is **red**. The Guild posts those last, for
those who have already proved themselves that day. A Red Rift was misgraded: what lives inside is older and worse than the mouth
suggested. And a Red Rift **closes behind those who enter**. There is no walking out. It
opens again when the Heart is dead, or when nobody inside is left standing.

Nobody knows why. What comes out of a Red Rift is worth the fear, and the Guild does not ask who is
willing.

## Goblins

| What people believe | What adventurers know |
|---|---|
| Goblins are stupid | One goblin is stupid. A nest learns |
| Goblins are weak | They are, which is why they never fight fair |
| Goblins are animals | They set traps, keep prisoners, and hold grudges |
| Goblins breed | No one has seen a goblin child. They come up from the Below grown |

A nest left alone does not only grow in number. Its goblins **change**: one takes to
wearing bones and the others start listening to it; one grows to twice the size of the
rest; one picks up a fallen knight's sword and, in time, something of the knight's
manner. The Guild calls these **castes** and posts bounties by caste, because the caste
tells you how long the nest has been left to ripen.

| Caste | What the nest's age made of it |
|---|---|
| Runt, slinger | A young nest |
| Skirmisher, trapper | A nest that has met adventurers and survived |
| Shaman, wolf rider | A nest that has stillstone to spare |
| Hobgoblin, hexer | A nest nobody dared enter for a season |
| Champion, paladin | A nest that has eaten good adventurers |
| Lord | A nest about to spill |

## The Frontier

The first region. A valley at the edge of the settled lands: one walled town, a fort at
the far end of the road, farms in between that are rebuilt more often than they are
harvested. The knights of the crown do not ride for a burned farm. The Guild does, for a
fee.

## The adventurer

You are nobody. You signed a ledger, were handed a chip of wood, and were told which gate
to leave by. You are not chosen, not marked, not the last of anything. If you become a
legend it will be by coming back, again and again, from places people do not come back
from.

## Threads left open on purpose

| Question | Why leave it open |
|---|---|
| What is the Below, and is anything down there sending the goblins? | The long story of the world; later regions |
| Why does the Hush exist? | Elite zones, where it behaves differently |
| Why do some Rifts turn red? | A reason to enter them |
| Why do old goblins come to resemble the adventurers they killed? | The paladin caste; elite capture of skills |
| Were there Rifts before the Guild, or did mining wake them? | The Guild's own guilt |

## What the lore explains in the game

| Game rule | In the world |
|---|---|
| The world moves only when the player acts | The Hush |
| Towns run in real time, without ticks | Walls keep the Hush out |
| Goblins are the only enemy | The Below sends nothing else |
| Castes grade difficulty | A nest ripens |
| Dungeons are different each time, zones are not | A Rift is new; the valley is old |
| Leaving a dungeon loses it | A Rift does not wait for you, and re-forms |
| Defeat costs the expedition, not the adventurer | The Guild drags its own out. It is in the contract |
| Guild ranks gate content | The Guild decides who enters which Rift |
| Old goblins use adventurers' skills; elite skills are captured from bosses | Nests learn from those they killed |
| The reagent for delicate work | Stillstone |
| Each adventurer has their own Rifts | The Below opens for the one who looks for it. Two adventurers at the same crack in the rock do not find the same nest |
| Boss trophy | Heartstone |

## Naming rule

The game is in English for the first version and multilingual afterwards. Names must be
**short** (one or two syllables when possible), made of common words, easy to say and to
keep as they are in other languages: Rift, Hush, Heart, Onyx.

## Names to avoid

The design must not use the vocabulary of its inspirations. In particular, from the
second: its word for a dungeon's entrance, its word for the people who clear them, its
letter grades, its name for the stones, its name for the moment monsters escape, its
colour-named sealed dungeon as a proper noun. From the first: every character title and
the names of its ranks in their order. Our ladder has ten grades, as the first inspiration's does, and shares metals with many
works; its materials, its order and its last grade are ours (see design/06).

| Concept | Our working name | Alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| The place goblins come from | The Below | The Under, the Deep |
| A dungeon's entrance | **Rift** (owner's choice) | Breach |
| Time standing still | The Hush | The Stillness, the Held Breath |
| The ore | Stillstone | — |
| A boss | Heart | Root, Marrow |
| Goblins escaping an uncleared dungeon | Spill | Pour, Rising |
| A sealed, misgraded dungeon | Red Rift | Shut Rift |
| Those who grade dungeons | Assayers | Surveyors |
